Nursery, LKG & UKG Interview Questions – Complete Preparation Guide

LKG admission interviews are not exams. Schools check basic communication, recognition of colors and numbers, listening skills, and social comfort. Children are asked simple questions about their name, family, favourite things, counting, shapes, animals, and rhymes. Parents may also be asked about the child’s routine and behaviour. Preparation should be playful, short, and stress-free. Confidence matters more than perfect answers.

 

 

What Schools Really Check in Nursery, LKG & UKG Interviews

Many parents feel nervous about admission interviews. But the truth is simple. Schools are not testing intelligence.

They mainly observe:

  • Can the child say their name and age?
  • Can they follow simple instructions?
  • Do they recognise basic colours and numbers?
  • Are they comfortable sitting without parents for a few minutes?
  • Do they respond when spoken to?

Even shy children get selected. Schools look at readiness for group learning, not perfection.

Common LKG Interview Questions for a Child With Sample Answers

Below are the most common LKG interview questions asked during admission interactions.

Personal Questions

  • What is your name?
  • How old are you?
  • When is your birthday?
  • Can you introduce yourself?
  • Where do you live?

Sample answer: My name is Riya. I am four years old.

Short sentences are perfectly fine.

Family-Related Questions

  • What is your father’s name?
  • What is your mother’s name?
  • What does your father do?
  • Do you have brothers or sisters?
  • What is your sibling’s name?

Children can answer: My father’s name is Rahul.

No need for long explanations.

Questions About Favourite Things

  • What is your favourite colour?
  • What is your favourite toy?
  • What is your favourite cartoon?
  • What is your favourite food?
  • What do you like to play?
  • Who is your best friend?

Answer: My favourite colour is blue.

Basic Concept Questions

These are very common in LKG admission interview questions.

Action-Based Questions: Listening Skills

Teachers may say:

  • Stand up.
  • Sit down.
  • Clap your hands.
  • Touch your nose.
  • Bring me the red block.
  • Give me a high-five.

This checks listening and attention.

Rhymes and Speaking

Children may be asked:

  • Can you say a rhyme?
  • Can you sing a song?
  • Can you tell a short poem?

One rhyme is enough:
Twinkle Twinkle
Johny Johny
Baa Baa Black Sheep

Schools only check confidence and clarity.

Nursery Interview Questions for Admission

Kindergarten interviews are usually even simpler.

Common nursery interview questions include:

  • What is your name?
  • Show me your hands.
  • Where are your eyes?
  • What colour is this?
  • Can you clap?
  • Can you say A B C?
  • Can you count till 5?
  • What toy do you like?

For nursery, behaviour matters more than answers.

UKG Admission Interview Questions

UKG interview questions may be slightly advanced compared to LKG.

You may see:

  • Count from 1 to 20.
  • Identify numbers written on paper.
  • Recognise small and capital letters.
  • Match pictures.
  • Simple opposite words: big–small
  • Identify days of the week.
  • Read simple three-letter words.
  • Write your name.

UKG interviews check school readiness more clearly but still remain age-appropriate.

LKG Admission Interview Questions for Parents (With Sample Answers)

Parents are usually asked 4–8 simple questions.

Common questions:

  • Tell us about your child’s daily routine.
  • How does your child behave with other children?
  • Is your child toilet trained?
  • Does your child understand English?
  • Has your child attended play school?
  • What are your child’s strengths?
  • What are your expectations from the school?

Answer for routine:
He wakes up at 7 AM, goes to play school, plays in the evening, and we read a short story before bed.

Keep answers:

  • Honest
  • Positive
  • Child-focused

Avoid exaggeration.

How to Prepare Your Child for LKG Interview: Step-by-Step Plan

Preparation should feel natural.

1. Daily 5-Minute Practice

Practice:

  • Name
  • Age
  • Parents’ names
  • One rhyme

Do not extend beyond 10 minutes.

2. Use Play Instead of Drilling

  • Count toys while playing.
  • Identify colours using crayons.
  • Use picture books for animals.
  • Ask simple questions during meals.

Children learn better through play.

3. Conduct Mock Interviews at Home

2–3 times a week is enough.

Structure:

  1. Greet properly.
  2. Ask 8–10 simple questions.
  3. One rhyme.
  4. End positively.

Stop while your child is still happy.

4. Encourage Sentence Answers

If child says: Blue.

You can say: Yes, my favourite colour is blue.

Model gently. Do not force memorised answers.

5. Build Emotional Confidence

  • Visit school area before interview day.
  • Tell your child: Teacher just wants to talk.
  • Avoid showing your own anxiety.

Children copy your emotions.

Common Mistakes Parents Make

Turning Practice Into an Exam

Long sessions and repeated drilling create fear.

Keep it short and playful.

Creating Pressure

Saying things like: If you don’t answer, you won’t get admission

This reduces confidence.

Over-Coaching Memorised Speeches

Schools prefer natural answers.

Memorised answers may fail if question changes slightly.

Answering for the Child

Give time to think.

Let the child try first.

Interview Day Checklist

Before the interview:

  • Ensure proper sleep.
  • Give light breakfast.
  • Dress comfortably.
  • Reach early.
  • Stay calm.

Most importantly, smile and let your child respond independently.
